Winter in Spain, measured

Not “mild” as a brochure adjective. Mild as a count: how many nights the heating goes on, how many mornings there is frost on the car, how many days are warm enough to sit outside — at 838 AEMET weather stations, winter by winter, over 9 winters.

A Spanish winter runs here from 1 November to 31 March: 151 days. And it is not one winter but many: at the median station 30 of those 151 days are warm enough to sit outside, while Tuineje, Puerto Gran Tarajal manages all 151. That spread is the whole point — which is why the useful question is not “how is winter in Spain” but how is winter where you are going.

137
nights below 10 °C at that median station — the ones you heat. At Yaiza Playa Blanca it is none at all

There are no shoulder months. November and March are 40.4 % of the winter and take 37.2 % of the heating nights — almost exactly their share. The cold is not concentrated in December-February; it is spread. Frost is the exception: 22.8 % of it falls in those two months, a little over half their share. So an October-to-April stay is not two mild months wrapped around three hard ones.

How the cities compare

Eight Spanish cities side by side on frost nights, nights below 5 °C and nights below 10 °C. The gap between them is larger than the brochures suggest.

I am staying three months

For a long stay the median lies: what decides is the longest run with no day warm enough to sit outside. Across the mainland and Balearics that run is 90 days at the median — and only eight stations keep it short without freezing you.

I want both halves of the year

Mild winter and a summer you can sleep through are close to incompatible on the mainland: the correlation is −0.70. Two stations in all of Spain manage both, and both are in the Canaries.

What “mild” is worth as a label

The Mild Winter seal goes to the stations that clear a fixed bar rather than the ones that rank highest: it is a threshold, not a league table, so a place either clears it or does not. 67 hold it.

Everything on these pages comes from AEMET's official daily records — the day's high and low at a named station. Nothing is interpolated, modelled or averaged across neighbours. Where a figure is a median we say so, and where the extreme matters more than the middle we publish the extreme.
