What the five numbers mean

All of them come from the daily records of AEMET, Spain’s national weather service, and all of them are the median of the series — never the mean. Averaging a brutal winter with a gentle one invents a mild one that never happened.

  • Heating nights: nights below 10 °C between 1 November and 31 March, out of 151. This is the number a monthly average hides.
  • Terrace days: days that reach 18 °C, in the same window. What you actually came for.
  • Rainy days: days with 1 mm or more. Over a three-month stay this decides more than a degree either way.
  • Frost nights: nights below 0 °C. For a lot of people this is a yes-or-no question, not a number.
  • Tropical nights: nights that never drop below 20 °C, counted over the whole calendar year — not just summer, because a fifth of them fall outside it. This is the price of a mild winter, and it is the one nobody quotes you.

Every row is one real thermometer. Nothing is interpolated and no stations are averaged together: if your town has no station, the nearest one is a neighbour’s reading, not yours. Where a city has several stations they can differ a lot — that gap is the urban heat island, not an error.
